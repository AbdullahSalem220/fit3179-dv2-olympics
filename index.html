Australia at the Olympic Games, 1896–2026

Green and Gold

Australia has won 594 Summer and 25 Winter Olympic medals. This is the story of where they came from, who won them, and how a country of 27 million keeps punching above its weight.

By Abdullah Salem · October 2026

01 Paris 2024

Paris 2024 was Australia’s best Olympics for gold medals. The team won 18 gold, 19 silver and 16 bronze and finished fourth on the medal table, behind only the United States, China and Japan. Its 53 medals were second only to the 58 won at the Sydney Games in 2000.

02 Punching above our weight

The medal table favours big countries with big teams. Share the medals out by population, though, and Australia stands out: it won the eighth most medals per person in Paris, more than any other country of its size or larger.

03 Rise, fall and rebound

Australia’s rise has not been smooth. After strong results in the 1950s and 1960s, the team came home from Montreal in 1976 without a single gold medal. The Australian Institute of Sport opened in Canberra in 1981, and by the Sydney Games in 2000 Australia was winning more medals than ever before.

04 Where the Games took us

Australian athletes have won medals at Games on five continents. Both home Games set records: Melbourne 1956’s 35 medals were not beaten until 1996, and Sydney 2000’s 58 is still the most Australia has won at any Games.

05 Our strongest sports

More than a third of all Australia’s Summer medals have come from the pool. But Australians have won medals in 28 different sports, and newer events such as skateboarding and surfing are already adding to the count.

06 Who wins the medals

Women now win most of Australia’s medals. In Paris, women’s events brought home 30 medals and men’s events 20. Australia’s most successful Olympian is swimmer Emma McKeon, with 14 medals.

07 Where our medallists were born

Most medallists were born in or near a capital city. Measured against population, the smaller states and territories produce a surprising number, although small numbers make their rates jump around. Another 77 medallists were born overseas, most often in the United Kingdom or South Africa.

Birth state is where an athlete was born, not where they trained or live.

08 Summer and Winter

Australia is a summer sports nation, but its Winter team keeps improving. Milano Cortina 2026 brought 6 medals, including 3 gold, its best Winter Games so far.

09 Next stop: Brisbane 2032

From a single athlete in Athens in 1896 to a team of 461 in Paris, Australia has become one of the world’s leading Olympic nations. In 2032 Brisbane will host the Summer Games, the third time for Australia after Melbourne in 1956 and Sydney in 2000. Both of those home Games gave Australia its best medal total up to that time.

Methodology

  • Medal counts follow the official medal table: a team event counts as one medal. Medals won as “Australasia” with New Zealand (1908 and 1912) and by teams mixing Australians with other nations are not counted.
  • Rank is the place on the medal table, which is ordered by gold medals, then silver, then bronze.
  • Sports are grouped, so track, road and BMX cycling all count as Cycling. Women’s and men’s medals are counted by the event’s category.
  • Birthplaces are placed on the map only when the town name matches exactly one place in that state. 805 of 820 Australian-born medallists (98%) could be placed. The state map covers 530 of the 610 medallists of 2000–2024 and uses each state’s population in March 2026.
  • Overseas births: 77 medallists were born overseas; 108 have no recorded birthplace. Paris 2024 medallists were linked to earlier Games by name and birth date.
  • Maps use equal-area projections, which keep countries and states at their true relative size.

Full details, lookups and the scripts that prepare the data are in the project README.
